## Progress

- **Blade counts (G1).** The inference that [74]'s four propellers differ was a misattribution:
  ref. 26 is the THRUST-CURVE source, not the propeller source. [74] states its own provenance
  (refs 29/30). Converging evidence — the Aeronaut Cam Carbon naming rule, "break" as a
  lift-distribution station, the ref.-29 design tool validated on two-bladed designs, and ref. 26's
  propellers being 9.4 in against [74]'s 13 in — says all four are two-bladed. **Qualifier (c) is
  probably lost, which makes the G1 wording decision easy: drop "at fixed blade count".**

- **Strouhal 0.28 — provenance closed at the primary source.** Chain traced and every link read:
  Roshko 1961 JFM -> Yudin TM 1136 -> Schlegel TR 66-4 -> Marte & Kurtz TR 32-1462. Roshko measured
  **0.27** (mean 0.267) at **R > 3.5e6**; small propellers run at Re_c 3e4-9e4. The constant is
  applied 40-100x below its measured Reynolds number, on a circular cylinder, in a regime where
  Roshko himself reports ~0.2. The rival "it fails because the blade rotates" explanation is
  eliminated (Weiss, Jaroslawski, Fava all find no rotational effect on transition).

- **Recirculation onset for a mounted rotor — found, and worse than assumed.** Five agents called it
  unpublished; it is in NASA NTRS. Pettingill 2020: "less than one second" of clean data unmitigated,
  "at least 2 seconds" with mesh screens — below the manuscript's 2.5-7.5 s floor — and it corrupts
  the 4th/8th/12th/16th shaft harmonics, not just broadband.

- **Propagation gap half-closed.** Fan et al. 2026 reports the coarse tonal-vs-cepstral split for
  rotorcraft (harmonics 78-96%, MFCC down to 1.47%). Narrows rather than falls: everything it tracks
  sits below 1.4 kHz, where absorption does nothing. Its stated and reported distances do not
  reconcile — the split is citable, the ranges are not.
