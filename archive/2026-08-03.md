### The finding

Static ground acoustic detection and bearing is mature and fielded at national
scale (~6° from an eight-element MEMS array; 14 000 Sky Fortress nodes
reportedly carrying a fifth of Ukraine's air picture). Onboard drone audition
is a decade-old research field but 82 % of it is search-and-rescue pointed at
the ground from a hovering aircraft. Acoustic homing by an interceptor does not
yet exist in the open literature: the best published air-to-air result detects
*presence* at 0.3–2 m from a hovering platform and returns no bearing at all,
against one vendor's claim of a 16-microphone seeker at 100 m.

Four physical constraints derived in the paper and not addressed anywhere in
the acoustic literature: an information age of R/c, a target-motion bearing
bias of arctan(v/c) that is independent of range, a platform-motion aberration
of up to v/c (6.3° at 135 km/h), and an engagement budget of ~1.5 s.

### Evidence regime

Five tiers used throughout — [V] read at source + Crossref-checked, [C]
identifier-verified but abstract-only, [P] press/vendor claim, [D] derived here
with arithmetic shown, [G] searched and found nothing. This [V] is the weaker
one, not the adversarial three-vote [V] of Shroud_Comparison; stated in §II of
the paper.

### Bugs found and fixed while verifying

- **Years were wrong** — `max(year)` on page 1 picked up AIP/publisher *download
  stamps*, dating a 2014 paper to 2026. Rewrote as a context-scored picker (©,
  parenthesised year, received/accepted); when only bare numbers exist it now
  declines to guess rather than grabbing a postcode. Pendry 2000, Veselago 1968
